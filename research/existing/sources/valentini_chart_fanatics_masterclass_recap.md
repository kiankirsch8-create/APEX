2. Why 90% of Retail Structure & Price-Action Traders Lose (02:20 – 07:20)

• The Retail "Market Structure" Fallacy: Most retail traders learn concepts like Market Structure Shift (MSS), Break of Structure (BOS), Change of Character (CHoCH), Order Blocks, and Fair Value Gaps (FVGs) (2:48). They spot a break of a swing low/high, mark a supply/demand zone, and blindly enter on the retracement expecting a 1:3 trend continuation (2:56).
• Why It Fails Statistically (The Bell Curve Trap): Fabio explains that if you plot a Volume Profile over the day or dealing range, volume forms a Gaussian bell curve (3:42). When a retail breakout/retracement pattern forms inside the established bell curve (the Fair Value Area), statistical distribution dictates that 70% of the time price will remain trapped inside fair value and only 30% of the time will it break through (3:58). Retail traders end up taking 4–5 consecutive stop-losses trying to catch a "liquidity sweep" or "spring," turning what looked like a 1:10 trade into a net 1:2 or a loss (5:59).
• Auction Market Theory (AMT) Foundation: The financial market is a continuous two-way auction that cycles between two states (5:20):
	1. Balance (Fair Value): Buyers and sellers agree on price and transact efficiently back and forth within a defined range (the default state of the market) (5:29).
	2. Imbalance (Out of Balance): One side overwhelms the other, forcing price out of the value area to seek a new equilibrium (5:29).
• Why He Calls It a "Model" Instead of a "Strategy": A rigid strategy forces fixed rules onto a dynamic market that changes daily based on macro catalysts (for example, the market gapping down heavily on a Monday after US strikes on three sites in Iran) (6:31). Simply adding one filter—refusing to take trend-following trades until the market is confirmed Out of Balance—immediately increases win rate by 20% to 30% (6:18).
