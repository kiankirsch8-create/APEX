## What the results teach

1. **The headline net (+1.40 pips/trade) is noise, not edge.** A 9-hour hold has a per-trade sd of about 35 pips,
   so 360 trades give a standard error of about 1.9 pips. +2.50 is t 1.3. The result rests on 2020 (COVID-year
   trends, +8.7) and 2021 is clearly negative. Net above cost is easy to get at this horizon by luck. It is the
   t that matters.
2. **The pre-registered dose-response failed.** Larger prior moves (\|z\| >= 1.5) gave a *smaller* continuation
   (+1.87 against +2.50). If delayed execution drove the effect, bigger information moves should carry more
   follow-through. Same pattern as R017: the conditioning variable does not scale the effect.
3. **The short side carries the edge, as R004 predicts.** Shorts +5.2, longs -0.2. R004 found EURUSD tends to fall
   in European hours whatever the prior day did (EU-hours short leg about +1.4 pips/5h). A 07:00-16:00 window picks
   up that drift on the short trades and pays it on the long trades. So the asymmetry points to the known
   time-of-day effect, not to continuation. I did not test a short-only version: that would be fitting a seen
   slice, and R004 already covers the drift.
4. **Overall.** At the daily horizon the cost problem goes away, but the noise problem takes its place. With one
   pair and four years, a daily-frequency effect needs about 4-5 pips per trade to reach t 3, and nothing in FX
   daily momentum suggests EURUSD has that.
