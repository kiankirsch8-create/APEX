## 1. Harness bug: stored timestamps are 1 hour late during US daylight time (not patched)

`scalping/data/histdata.py` converts HistData stamps as fixed EST (`est + 5h`, line 51). The source actually follows
**New York wall-clock time with DST**. So during US DST (second Sunday of March to first Sunday of November, about 65%
of days), stored "UTC" = true UTC + 1h. In winter the stamps are correct.

Evidence from the build data (minute mids, 2018-21, no outcomes involved):

| check | summer (US DST) | winter | expected if stamps were true UTC |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-Friday NFP (08:30 ET) mean \|1-min move\|, stored 12:30 / 13:30 | 1.94 / **12.17** pips | 0.91 / **14.40** | summer spike at 12:30 |
| Rollover spread spike (17:00 NY) | stored **22:00** (2.2 pips) | stored **22:00** (2.4) | summer spike at 21:00 |
| Hourly vol profile, stored 06h / 07h / 08h / 14h / 15h / 16h | 0.58 / 1.03 / 1.25 / 1.38 / 1.50 / 1.16 | 0.62 / 1.01 / 1.21 / 1.34 / 1.46 / 1.14 | summer profile shifted 1h earlier |
| R012 8-pip release trigger, 2019Q2 (a summer quarter), trades | 1 (old clock) vs **11** (corrected) | | |

What it means:
- Strategies that read the UTC hour directly (R001, R002, R014, R017-R027, the band fades) effectively ran on a fixed
  "NY minus 5h" clock. That tracks the sessions consistently, so their "UTC" hour labels are only true in winter.
  Their results stand. The `by_session` buckets are off by an hour in summer.
- Strategies that converted the stored stamp with `zoneinfo` were **misaligned by one hour on summer days**:
  R003 (London fix), R004c, R008 (pre-fix), R009, R010 (London open), R011, R012 (releases), R015 (10:00 NY cut),
  R016, R025 (fix). R013 (Tokyo fix) used a fixed UTC hour, so in summer it was also one hour off true 00:55 UTC.
  For event-timed ideas (R003, R008, R012, R013, R015, R025), about two thirds of the sample was not at the event.
  **Those closures are weaker evidence than the ledger suggests.**
- The engine's funding at stored 22:00 = 17:00 NY all year. That is accidentally right.

Fix (for Kian, core file, not touched): treat the source stamp as New York wall time, i.e.
`stamp_naive.tz_localize("America/New_York", ambiguous=..., nonexistent=...).tz_convert("UTC")` instead of
`est + 5h`. Re-import, re-run the tests, then decide which event ideas to re-test. The importer docstring's
month-boundary note (files start at 05:00 UTC) should be re-checked with the same change. A test that checks the
NFP spike sits at 12:30 UTC in July and 13:30 in January would catch a regression.

## 4. What the results teach

1. **The rollover drift is not a transient.** Moves of 2.5+ pips over 21:45-23:00 do not come back by 00:45. Edge is
   about zero, and the bigger trigger does not help clearly (+0.12, 129 trades). Thin-book mid moves either carry
   information (Sydney flow) or Tokyo's arrival does not reset them inside 1h45. Either way, finding 1's reversion
   does not concentrate here.
2. **The clock bug was real, and fixing it removed R012's positive sign.** With releases aligned, the 8-pip trigger
   finds 211 events instead of 136, and continuation over 60 min is *negative* (-1.39). The 4-pip version is -0.50.
   R012's +1.40 came mostly from summer windows that were an hour away from any release. Post-release continuation
   is not there in EURUSD 2018-21. The negative sign is t -1.3, so a fade is not registered.
3. The negative R028 result is a useful warning. Other event-timed closures (fixes, option cut, Tokyo fix) were
   tested on about one third real events. Their re-tests could come out either way, so they should not be
   re-opened on the hope that they flip positive. Re-test them only if the importer is fixed, all at once and
   pre-registered, with the multiple-testing cost written down.
