## What the results teach

1. **Long M15 runs continue; they do not revert** (R021). Fading the run after its first opposite bar loses 0.54 pips
   per trade before cost at t -3.1, in every year, on both sides. The transient-impact decay that the metaorder
   literature describes is not visible at this horizon on EURUSD HistData, or it is swamped by persistence of the
   flow that produced the run. This fits the ledger's broader picture: session-scale and multi-hour moves look
   permanent (R005, R014, R020), and only *short* single-bar excursions revert (finding 1). The dose-response test
   pointed the wrong way for the mechanism (run >= 7: -0.62, more negative).
   The mirror image (run continuation) would earn about +0.5 pips pre-cost, half the round trip. I did not test it:
   it would be fitting a seen result, and +0.5 cannot clear ~1.05 pips of cost anyway.
2. **The limit-entry cost cut is mostly illusory in this fill model** (R022). The limit at the signal mid filled on
   92% of R017's signals (in high vol, price nearly always moves a further half spread inside 15 minutes). But:
   - a buy limit only fills once the *ask* reaches it, so at the fill the mid is a half spread lower and the half
     spread is still paid against the fill-time mid. The only cost actually removed is the 0.2 pip slippage
     (1.07 -> 0.87);
   - the trades it fills are the ones where price kept going, and the fast snap-backs it misses were the best trades.
     The edge fell by 0.38 pips (1.02 -> 0.64), more than the 0.2 saved.
   Net went from -0.04 (market entry) to -0.22. So findings point 5's "about 0.35-0.4 pip structural cut" does not
   hold for a signal-following reversion entry: the half spread you avoid comes back as adverse selection. This is
   the same warning as R001/R006, now measured on the best signal in the ledger.
3. **Consequence for the cost question in earlier reports.** "R017 would net +0.3 on a ~0.6-0.7 pip account" assumed
   cheaper *pricing* (tighter spread), not cheaper *order type*. R022 shows the order-type route does not get there.
   The question for Kian stays a broker question, not a strategy question.

- **Cost accounting for limit fills (not a bug, but worth knowing):** `costs_pips` on a limit fill still includes the
  half spread, measured against the mid at the fill tick, because the fill condition is ask <= level (buy). The
  "limit orders fill at their price, no slippage" line in `_template.py` is accurate, but the findings' estimate of a
  0.35-0.4 pip saving from limit entries over-states it: only the 0.2 pip slippage is saved unless the level sits
  inside the spread at fill time. Worth a sentence in the findings README.
