## What each result teaches

1. **Unscheduled M1 shocks do not revert** (R011). Win rate is 63% because TP (0.5x body) is half the SL (1x body), but
   the pre-cost edge is +0.14 (t 0.7) and no year, session or side has |t| > 1.3. Large non-news moves in HistData
   EURUSD behave like permanent impact, not inventory pressure, at a 30-minute horizon. This also means finding 1's
   reversion does **not** scale with shock size at the tail: it is a property of ordinary small moves.
2. **Post-release continuation has the predicted sign but no power** (R012). The 8-pip version has edge +1.40, which
   would just clear cost, but on 136 trades with a ~20 pip std the t is 0.8, and the years swing from +5.3 to -3.7.
   With a lower trigger the effect shrinks to +0.43, consistent with small releases carrying little information.
   Reaching t >= 3 at +1.4 pips would need roughly 2,000 large-release trades, about 60 years of EURUSD alone.
3. **Process note (my mistake):** I fixed R012's 8-pip trigger without checking how often it fires. A one-quarter
   frequency check (2019Q2: 1 of 260 release windows >= 8 pips, 9 >= 5 pips; no outcomes looked at) showed it could not
   reach 200 trades, so before running anything I added r012b (4-pip trigger) and logged it as eval 2 with the reason in
   its docstring and the ledger note. Next time: check event frequency **before** registering the threshold.
4. **FOMC slot (+13 pips, 12 trades, t 1.8)** is a single slice among many; not registered, not iterated. It is noted
   only because it fits the mechanism (the biggest scheduled information event); a test would need far more events
   than one pair over four years provides.
