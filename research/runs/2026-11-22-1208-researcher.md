## Ideas and why

Both ideas test a **new state variable** on the finding-1 / R017 M15 band fade (20 bars, 2 sd, market entry at bar
close, TP at the mean, 1:1 SL, 2h time stop, skip 20:45-23:15 UTC). Earlier runs tried session, band width, absolute
vol (R017), trend (R018), autocorrelation (R019) and level proximity. Only absolute vol moved the edge, up to about
cost, and its dose-response failed. This run tried two variables that the liquidity-provision literature says should
be closer to the real driver.

| id | idea | built on |
|---|---|---|
| R023 | Fade only when **quoted spreads are wide for the time of day** (spread_close vs the same-slot median over 20 days, mean of the last 4 ratios >= 1.15) | Inventory models (Ho-Stoll; Hendershott-Menkveld 2014): the reversal premium is highest when dealer capacity is scarce, and a wide spread measures that directly. Nearly independent of R017's vol (corr 0.08 on raw build spreads) |
| R024 | Fade only when **2h realised vol is high relative to its usual level at that time of day** (v8 / same-slot median >= 1.40, ~p80). Pre-registered eval 2 (run regardless, dose-response): >= 1.70 (~p90) | Nagel (2012): the premium follows liquidity *shocks* (unexpected vol). R017's absolute vol mixes the predictable intraday pattern and slow regimes (2020) with shocks. corr 0.34 with R017's vol96 |

Thresholds for both came from the distribution of the conditioning variable alone over 2018-21. No outcomes were
looked at before registration.

## R023: closed without evaluation (my pre-registration error)

The 2019Q2 sanity run gave 5 trades where about 70 were expected. Cause: the engine floors every spread the
strategy sees at the 0.7 pip broker minimum (`scalping/market.py: with_min_spread`, applied in `engine.py` when
`min_spread_points > 0`). I set the thresholds on **raw** HistData spreads (median 0.3 pip, quantised at 0.1). Under
the floor the ratio is about 1.0 almost always. The state only fires when the raw spread is above 0.7 pip: about 5% of
bars, concentrated 23:00-01:00 UTC (post-rollover) and 13:00 UTC (US data). That is a different variable, and those
windows are already covered by R001 and R011/R012. Running it would have spent an eval on a test that cannot answer
the question (and would fail the 200-trade gate), so I closed it with 0 evals. The mechanism stays **untested**: it
needs broker-quality spread data. The floor is right for costs, so this is not a harness bug, but strategies cannot
use spread dynamics below 0.7 pip.

## What the results teach

1. **Surprise volatility does not pick out bigger reversals.** Normalising vol by its time-of-day level gives *less*
   edge (+0.38) than R017's absolute vol (+1.02). It sits close to the unconditional M15 band fade (+0.32). So the
   part of R017's lift that is real comes from the *level* of vol, which makes every pip move bigger, not from
   unexpected liquidity demand. That agrees with the R017 and R018b readings in earlier reports.
2. **Dose-response is flat within noise.** Going from p80 to p90 adds +0.18 pips, well inside one standard error
   (about 0.6), and the gain is all 2020. That is not the scaling the mechanism predicts.
3. **The short-side asymmetry keeps coming back** (R017, R018b, R020, R022, R024): shorts about +1.1 to +1.3, longs
   about 0. R004 explains this as EURUSD's European-hours drift, not reversion. It has now shown up in five ideas,
   always as an unregistered slice. If it is ever tested, it has to be a fresh pre-registered idea about the drift
   itself (R004 is closed at +0.97, t 1.9). It must not be a short-only cut of a seen result.
4. **State-variable search on the M15 band fade looks exhausted.** Seven state variables have now been tried. None
   gets the edge clearly above the ~1.05 pip round trip, and none shows dose-response.
