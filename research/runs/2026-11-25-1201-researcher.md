## 1. Idea and why

| id | idea | built on |
|---|---|---|
| R031 | **Time-of-day return periodicity.** Each weekday hour slot (stored 07..19) is traded at its first bar in the sign of its own trailing 20-day mean slot return, when that mean has \|t\| >= 2.0. Market entry, hold to the next hour boundary, 30-pip catastrophe SL. Eval 2 (pre-registered, runs regardless, dose-response): \|t\| >= 2.5, predicted larger edge per trade | Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka (2010): returns in an intraday interval are positively autocorrelated with the same interval on prior days, attributed to recurring institutional flow at fixed clock times (scheduled rebalancing, execution algorithms, hedging desks). Uses the stored clock only. Slots are defined on that clock, so the importer bug just relabels slots (stored = NY wall + 5h,
which also tracks London local time through both DST regimes). Sanity run on 2019Q2: 30 trades, no warnings, no code
change after it. Under the null (no periodicity), \|t\| >= 2 with 19 df fires about 6% of the time, so about 500 trades
were expected without any signal. The actual count was 622.

## 3. What the results teach

1. **No hourly periodicity at a size one pair can resolve.** The edge per trade does rise with the threshold (0.64 to 1.27),
   as the dose-response predicted. But both t-values are 1.2-1.5. Eval 2's whole P&L comes from 2018Q2, the
   April-June 2018 EUR sell-off, when many slots had the same negative drift at once. In that quarter the rule
   is just hourly trend-following. Outside 2018 the edge is about 0.1-0.7 pips, and it turns negative in 2021.
   So the mechanism (slot-specific recurring flow) isn't what produced the gains.
2. **Signal count is close to the null rate.** 622 trades against about 500 expected from noise means most signals are
   chance \|t\| > 2 slots. With 20 observations per slot and an hourly sd of about 10-15 pips, a real 1-pip slot drift
   has t ≈ 0.4, so it's undetectable within one quarter. Block-local data (no warmup, 20-day burn-in each quarter,
   one-month blocks give zero trades) makes it worse.
3. **The stored 16-20 "late NY" slice has now shown up in three unrelated ideas** (R029 reversion t 3.0-3.5, R031 continuation
   t 2.4/3.1). Note: the stored clock = NY wall + 5h, which equals London local time + 0h in both winter and summer, so
   stored 16:00 is the London 4pm fix all year. The two ideas take opposite directions (fade versus follow), so this is
   not one effect. It's more likely a volatility artefact: an hour with large moves inflates any slice's |edge|. Not
   registered. A future idea on that window would need its own mechanism and would carry the multiple-testing cost of
   31 sliced ideas.
