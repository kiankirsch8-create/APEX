## Ideas and why

Both ideas keep the finding-1 M15 band fade (20 bars, 2 sd, market entry at bar close, TP at the mean, 1:1 SL,
2h time stop, skip 20:45-23:15 UTC, same as R017) and test a **state variable** that a mechanism says should pick out
the trades where the reversal is larger. R017 showed the volatility state lifts the edge to about cost (+1.02) but its
dose-response failed, so this run tried two other state variables.

| id | idea | built on |
|---|---|---|
| R018 | Fade only excursions **against the 5-day trend** (buy dips when mean20 > mean480, sell rallies when mean20 < mean480). Eval 2, set at registration: same plus R017's high-vol regime (vol96 >= 5.4 pips; disclosed as building on R017's seen result) | Permanent vs temporary price impact. Ledger R003/R005/R009/R014: hour-to-session moves look permanent; FX time-series momentum at daily-weekly horizons (Menkhoff et al. 2012; Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012). A short move against the slow trend should more often be uninformed liquidity demand, so it should revert more |
| R019 | Fade only when the trailing 480-bar lag-1 **autocorrelation of M15 returns is <= -0.05** (bottom tercile of the variable alone over 2018-21, 31-35% of time in every year). Conditional eval 2 (ac <= -0.10) only if eval 1 edge >= +0.8 | Nagel (2012), Hendershott-Menkveld (2014): the reversal premium is time-varying and persistent. Measured recent reversal should be a more direct state proxy than vol |

The R019 threshold came from the distribution of the conditioning variable over 2018-21 (p10 -0.10, p33 -0.05,
median -0.02, p67 +0.01); no outcomes were looked at. Sanity runs on 2019Q2 before the first evals (R018 157 trades,
R019 111 trades); no code change after them.

## What the results teach

1. **Trend direction does not pick out the reverting excursions** (R018). Counter-trend fades earn +0.22, about the
   same as the unconditional M15 band fade in the findings (+0.32). If moves against the 5-day trend were mostly
   temporary impact, this half should have shown a clearly larger edge. It did not, in any year.
2. **R018b is R017 again, within noise.** High vol plus counter-trend gives +1.20 against R017's +1.02 for high vol
   alone. The difference (+0.18) is well inside one standard error (about 0.65 pips here). The edge rests on 2020
   (COVID volatility: +2.30), and 2019 is negative. Net +0.14 is the first positive net in a while, but on 636 trades
   with t 1.8 and 47% positive quarters it is not a result. The pre-registered prediction (eval 2 > eval 1) held
   only because the vol filter was added, not because of the trend condition.
3. **Measured recent reversal does not predict future reversal** (R019). In the most-reverting third of the time the
   band fade earns -0.07, *below* the unconditional edge. So at M15 over days, the "reversal premium" is not a
   persistent state that you can read off recent returns. Part of this is estimation noise (the standard error of a
   480-return autocorrelation is about 0.046, close to the spread of the variable itself), but that is also why it
   cannot be used as a filter.
4. **Overall picture after 19 ideas.** Of every state variable tried on the finding-1 reversion signal (session,
   band width, vol, trend, autocorr, level proximity), only volatility moves the pip edge, and only up to about the
   round-trip cost. Volatility helps because every pip move gets bigger, not because the reversal premium gets
   richer per unit of risk (R017 dose-response, R018b).
